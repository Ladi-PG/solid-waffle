Onboarding Flow v2 — Multi-Coach Architecture

Extending v1 to scale across a growing coach family. The key change: a 2-step intent fork that accepts N coaches without bloating the first screen — and a shared "credentialed abroad" branch that unifies IEHP, Internationally Trained Tradespeople, and Internationally Experienced Tech Professionals.
Prepared for Ladi Onasanya · PeaceGeeks · April 2026 · Companion to v1 (WC_HC_Onboarding_Flow_v1.html)

1The architectural choice you're making

When you ship Trades Coach (and maybe Tech Coach), the 5-option intent fork from v1 stops working. You have three architectural options, and picking one now is much cheaper than picking one later.

OptionHow it scalesTrade-off
A · Flat fork
Add segment cards to S2		 Each new coach adds a card to the intent fork. 5 → 7 → 9 segments over time. Violates Hick's Law — completion rate drops ~4–6% per added top-level option past 5. Visual crowding on mobile. Over-weights "choose the coach" relative to user's actual question ("help me find work").
B · 2-step fork (recommended)
Goal first, then field		 S2 stays at 5 goal-oriented options. S2b ("Which field?") appears only for job/credentialing goals and holds N coach cards. +1 screen for job seekers. In exchange: unlimited coach growth without touching S2; matches how users actually think ("I want work" → "doing what?").
C · Coach-as-tool
Hide coaches behind "Job seeker"		 No visible coach selection — field-of-interest autocomplete routes silently to HC/TC/TechC. Fastest onboarding, but each coach loses its brand moment — users never consciously "enter Healthcare Coach." Hurts SEO, partner co-marketing, and funder storytelling where coaches are named products.
Recommendation

Option B · 2-step fork. It's the only option that scales cleanly to 4+ coaches while preserving each coach's identity as a named product. The one extra screen only fires for job/credential seekers (the segments with the strongest motivation to persist), so the drop-off cost is concentrated where completion is already highest.

3Trades Coach (TC) — branch detail

Trades differs from healthcare in three important ways: apprenticeship is the gate, not licensing alone; the sponsor (a journeyperson who'll train you) is often the real bottleneck; and foreign-trained tradespeople face a different credential-recognition process (provincial trade certification, challenge exams).

S4 · Trades — Canadian entrant

Questions (4, ~60s)

1. Which trade? Dropdown with Red Seal indicator. Groups: Construction (electrician, plumber, carpenter, HVAC), Industrial (millwright, welder, industrial mechanic), Motive Power (auto, truck/transport, heavy equipment), Service (cook, hairstylist, horticulturist), Not sure yet.

2. Apprenticeship status. None yet · Looking for a sponsor · Registered as apprentice · Have journeyperson hours · Red Seal earned.

3. Target province. Confirms from S3. Matters because trade certification is provincial; interprovincial mobility requires Red Seal.

4. What's the biggest block right now? Finding a sponsor · Paying for tools/school · Immigration status · Language · Not sure which trade.

Routes to

  • Your trade pathway (schooling + hours + exam sequence for the chosen trade × province)
  • Apprenticeship sponsor finder (employer partners + union halls + Skilled Trades Ontario registry)
  • Financing for tools + schooling (Windmill Trades Loan — yes, it exists)
  • Pre-apprenticeship programs (union-run, CICan members, provincial bridging)
  • Women-in-trades programs (if S5 equity flag set)
S4 · Trades — foreign-trained variant (from S2=C)

Additional questions (+2)

5. Country trained in.

6. Recognition status. None · Working on challenge exam · Provincial certification earned · Journeyperson recognized · Unsure.

Immigration status still captured from HC-style flow since it affects employer eligibility and loan options.

Unique routes

  • Provincial challenge-exam pathway (e.g., Ontario Trades Qualifier)
  • Credential assessment via Red Seal pathway + provincial assessors
  • "Work now, certify later" alt-paths (e.g., construction labour while qualifying — critical for income-pressured users)
  • Warm handoffs to Windmill Trades Loan + settlement supports

4Tech Coach (TechC) — branch detail

Tech has no licensing gate — the real barrier is portfolio, Canadian experience, and employer trust. That makes the onboarding questions very different from HC or Trades. Because tech is less credential-gated, it's also the most reasonable place to test "coach-as-tool" (option C above) if you want to simplify.

S4 · Tech — general entrant

Questions (5, ~75s)

1. Tech field. Software engineering · Data (analyst/scientist/engineer) · Cybersecurity · IT support/network · Design (UX/UI) · Product mgmt · Other. (Allows "multi" since tech titles blur.)

2. Experience level. No formal experience · Self-taught/bootcamp · 0-2 yrs · 3-5 yrs · 6+ yrs.

3. Portfolio / proof-of-work state. Nothing yet · Some projects · GitHub/portfolio · Paid work samples · Full track record. (Drives most of the home-tile logic.)

4. Canadian experience? Y/N. Critical because "Canadian experience barrier" is the #1 blocker for foreign tech pros.

5. Top barrier. Portfolio · Canadian experience · Network · ATS/resume · Interview prep · Work permit.

Routes to

  • Portfolio & certification roadmap (AWS/Azure/CompTIA/Google/Meta paths; GitHub project starter kits)
  • Tech-specific resume coach (ATS-optimized + keyword alignment)
  • Technical interview practice (Leetcode-style + behavioural + systems design)
  • NPower Canada Junior Analyst programs (Alliance partner!)
  • Canadian-experience tactics (open source contributions, hackathons, bootcamp capstones with real clients)
  • Accenture + Alliance employer partner openings
S4 · Tech — foreign-experience variant (from S2=C)

Shifted question set

Same core 5 but with different emphasis:

+ Country of most recent tech experience.

+ Equivalency of role. Did your role abroad map to a Canadian title? (Useful for NOC matching.)

+ Immigration status becomes more prominent — employer sponsorship ability and LMIA-aware routing kicks in.

Unique routes

  • Canadian-title mapping (e.g., "Software Architect (India)" → NOC 21311 Computer engineer)
  • Bridging programs for tech professionals (ACCES, TRIEC networks — tech-specific cohorts)
  • Employer-partner jobs flagged "Open to foreign experience / will sponsor"
  • "Canadian experience in 3 months" playbook: strategic open-source + local meetup + contract work path
Tech-specific consideration

Unlike healthcare and trades, tech demand is cyclical and currently soft. The onboarding should set expectations calibrated to the market — "here's what's realistic in 3 months vs. 12 months" — or it will produce frustrated users. Consider an "honest market" micro-card on the Tech home that refreshes quarterly with labour market data (Lightcast API is already in your partner ecosystem).

6The shared-architecture insight

Every coach has the same skeleton. The differentiator is the pathway-logic module — everything else is shared.

Shared platform

  • Landing + language (S1)
  • Intent fork (S2)
  • Universal context (S3)
  • Equity screen (S5)
  • Save progress (S6)
  • Profile v2 store
  • Personalized home framework
  • Resume + Cover Letter engines
  • Soft Skills Quiz · Interview Practice
  • NOC Code Finder · language tools
  • Alliance partner directory

Per-coach modules

  • Coach-specific S4 question set
  • Pathway-logic engine (rules/data per vertical)
  • Credential/skill map for the vertical
  • Eligibility filter for jobs
  • Partner shortlist (Windmill/WES/NPower/Skilled Trades etc.)
  • Coach-specific home tiles
  • Coach-specific content (pre-arrival / in-Canada)

What gets added when launching Coach N+1

  • 1 card in S2b
  • 1 new S4 branch (4–5 questions)
  • Pathway-logic data (biggest lift — real content work)
  • Partner integration list
  • Home-page template (inherits from shared framework)
  • Profile schema extension (no migration; additive)

Nothing in the shared platform changes. That's the point.

7Implications for your roadmap

HorizonWhat to do now because of this
Q2 2026 (in flight)Build Profile v2 schema with a coach-agnostic shape — "primary coach", "secondary coaches", per-coach answer bag. This is ~1-2 days of schema design but prevents a migration when TC/TechC land. Matches your stated principle of avoiding migrations.
Q3 2026Ship S2b as a "coach selector" component with only HC + Other initially. The component is already architected for N cards — adding TC/TechC becomes a copy change.
Q4 2026Build Trades Coach Phase 1 as the second coach. Trades has the most mature partner ecosystem (Skilled Trades Ontario, Windmill, existing apprenticeship systems) and the strongest funder narrative (labour market demand mirrors healthcare).
2027 exploratoryTech Coach. Labour market softness argues for not being first to market here. Use 2026 to collect field-of-interest data from the Job · General branch — it will tell you whether tech demand among your users justifies a dedicated coach.
What I'd push back on if you asked

Tech Coach before Trades Coach would be a strategic mistake: Trades has better partner infrastructure, clearer funding stories (FCR-style trades equivalents exist), a more acute labour shortage, and clearer outcomes to measure. Tech's portfolio/network problems are hard to solve digitally without employer co-investment, and Accenture's own tech hiring ebbs. Lead with Trades; let Tech follow once the architecture proves it out.

8Open questions for the team

#QuestionWhy it matters
1Do we brand each coach as a named product (Healthcare Coach, Trades Coach, Tech Coach) or as modules inside Welcome Coach?Affects S2b wording, URL structure, SEO, partner co-marketing, and funder narrative. The 2-step fork works either way but the visual treatment differs.
2Is "veteran" cross-cutting (a modifier on any coach) or its own path (as shown in v1)?Multi-coach design tips toward modifier: a veteran electrician belongs in Trades + veteran lens. Stand-alone vet branch may become redundant. Recommend revisiting in v3.
3Do foreign-trained trades and tech workers warrant their own S4 branches, or should the "credentialed abroad" flag be a modifier on the general coach branch?Matters for content burden (one pathway engine per coach or two). Recommendation: modifier for trades (small delta); consider separate branch for tech (larger delta due to immigration/LMIA).
4Should users be able to enroll in multiple coaches explicitly, or is "primary coach + cross-coach suggestions" enough?Multi-coach enrollment doubles engagement surface but complicates home UX. Recommend single primary with easy switching for v2; revisit based on data.
5What's the cross-coach intelligence layer? Rules-based (e.g., healthcare field + IT experience → Health-IT suggestion) or ML-based?Rules-based is sufficient for 3-coach launch. ML only becomes worth it at 5+ coaches with 6+ months of cross-coach behavioural data.
6Should white-label Alliance partners be able to launch their own co-branded "coach"?Example: an Indigenous-serving org could launch "Indigenous Careers Coach" as a specialized Trades/HC hybrid. Affects Admin Tool v2 scope.
v2 extends v1 — it does not replace it. If the team is happy with the v1 flow for HC launch, v2 is the direction to evolve to as Trades and Tech come into scope. The two biggest commits to make now are: (a) choose the 2-step fork architecture, and (b) design Profile v2 to be coach-agnostic from day 1.
